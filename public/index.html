Next-generation RAG assistants · for founders and CTOs

Discover and implement next-generation RAG assistants.

Retrieval-augmented assistants that answer from your own data and show where each answer came from. They are built as lightweight modules that sit beside your database rather than a platform you migrate to, with a one-week path from proof of concept to production, wherever your data lives.

Modules

Lightweight by design

Connect, index, retrieve, ground, cite, guard: six small modules you switch on as you need them, running in your cloud or ours, with nothing to rip out later.

1 week

Proof of concept to production

Day one scopes the questions and connects the data. Days two to four build and measure against your real questions. Day five hardens it, hands it over, and goes live.

Any DB

Wherever your data lives

Postgres, MySQL, SQL Server, MongoDB, Snowflake, BigQuery, ServiceNow, SharePoint, S3 or R2, on-prem, in your cloud, or managed. The data stays where it is; we bring retrieval to it.

Ask us directly ›

What do you need from us to get started?

Who it's for, what they need to get done, and where the experience should live. We begin by understanding that one task — the information it needs and the steps that take up people's time — before we design or build anything.

Do you only work with ServiceNow?

No. ServiceNow is one of our areas of expertise. We also build standalone mobile and web products, and connect agents to other systems through the interfaces and access those systems provide.

How fast can a RAG assistant go live?

In a week, when the data access is ready on day one. We scope the questions it must answer and connect the data on day one, build and measure it against your real questions over the next three days, and harden and hand it over on day five. It runs as small modules beside the database you already have, in your cloud or ours — see RAG assistants.

Will the agent act on its own?

Only inside limits you agree to. We make progress visible, explain what needs attention, and give people a clear way to review important actions before they happen. Those controls are defined around the task and the product.

How do you handle our data and systems?

Data access, guardrails, and review steps are agreed up front, before anything is connected. We then test complete tasks — including missing information and failed actions — before the product is used.

What happens after launch?

Before launch we agree how the product will run, who will maintain it, and how improvements will be measured — then we help you keep it running.

Are the examples on this page real?

They are illustrations built from invented data, and pressing their buttons changes nothing outside the page. For something you can use today, try Summaverick Research, which finds ServiceNow documentation and shows its sources.
